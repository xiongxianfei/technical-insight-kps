# Repeated reports can share one evidence base

## Key takeaway

Counting documents can overstate the number of independent observations supporting a claim.

## Summary

A study may appear in a paper, a review, guidance and many derivative summaries. Repetition can increase visibility without adding independent experimental support.

## Statement

Counting documents can overstate the number of independent observations supporting a claim.

## Explanation and derivation

Cochrane explicitly distinguishes a study from its reports. This package adapts the idea to engineering evidence families: one dataset, experiment, organization guidance chain or shared simulation can underlie several citations.

## Application example

Three handbook sections and two websites repeating one test do not equal five replicated tests. They may still contribute definitions, context or counterarguments; record those roles rather than discarding them or counting them as independent.

## Scope and counterevidence

Independence is not binary: different analyses can reveal weaknesses in shared data. A definition issued by the relevant authority may need one source. The aim is accurate attribution, not a fixed citation quota.
