# Decision preference depends on objectives and uncertainty

## Key takeaway

The same engineering facts can support different actions when objectives, constraints or uncertainty change.

## Summary

Evidence constrains the consequences of alternatives, but does not alone choose the desired consequences. A decision joins technical understanding with preferences, authority, costs and risk.

## Statement

The same engineering facts can support different actions when objectives, constraints or uncertainty change.

## Explanation and derivation

NASA decision guidance separates criteria, alternatives and uncertainty. The package therefore keeps insight confidence distinct from adoption status and records the goal that connects understanding to action.

## Application example

Greater thermal storage may be preferred for a brief burst, while lower thermal resistance may matter for continuous operation. A single “better cooling” label hides the objective needed to interpret the result.

## Scope and counterevidence

Required safeguards are not ordinary weighted preferences. Missing evidence cannot be made precise by a scoring formula. Extra research may be worthwhile only if it could change a consequential decision enough to justify cost or delay.
