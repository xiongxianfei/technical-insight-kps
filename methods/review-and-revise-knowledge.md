# Review and revise knowledge

## Key takeaway

Revise the claim and affected uses when new evidence changes its support or scope.

## Summary

Use after a contrary finding, changed context, source correction or practical result. A useful revision can strengthen, narrow, retire or leave a claim unresolved without erasing the original observation.

## Inputs and prerequisites

Versioned knowledge, new evidence or change request, dependency links and publication authority. Existing repositories are inspected before branch creation or replacement.

## Rationale

Principles, Models, Methods and Practices can share assumptions. A changed premise may leave some applications valid and others unsupported; mechanically rewriting every dependent paragraph loses this distinction.

## Procedure

1. Identify the specific claim and new contribution, with provenance and context.
2. Determine whether the change concerns evidence, explanation, applicability, selected action or authoring mechanics.
3. Inspect affected Models, Methods and inline Practice summaries. Retain faithful raw observations while changing interpretation when warranted.
4. Make the smallest coherent revision, record why and distinguish new assumptions from newly established facts.
5. Review self-containment, source contributions and semantic link consistency before refreshing dependency hashes.
6. Run publication tests and record limits. For an existing corresponding repository, open a ready-for-review PR; otherwise prepare a validated ZIP.
7. Do not merge, tag or create an official release without the project owner’s authorization. A green structure check is not a scientific approval.

## Expected effect

A coherent revision whose changed claims and downstream decisions are explicit.

## Validation and counterevidence

Check that obsolete advice is not left active in a Practice summary. Verify that revision did not silently convert uncertainty into a rule. Unchanged files need not receive artificial new knowledge versions.

## Limits

The methodology does not define every organization’s governance. Safety-critical changes remain subject to the applicable controls and expert review.
