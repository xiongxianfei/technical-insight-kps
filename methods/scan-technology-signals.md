# Scan technology signals

## Key takeaway

Capture what changed, why it might matter and what remains unverified before interpreting a technology trend.

## Summary

Use horizon scanning when promising approaches or emerging constraints may be missing from the current view. The output is a dated signal register and a small investigation queue. This engineering adaptation draws on the UK Futures Toolkit; it is not a prediction engine or a complete technology census.

## Inputs and prerequisites

A decision, functional need, time horizon, scope, search budget and authorized public sources. Define languages, geography, dates and excluded material. Do not upload confidential engineering questions to public search or AI services without permission.

## Rationale

A search limited to current suppliers and familiar vocabulary can omit alternative mechanisms. Broad scanning can expose leads, but attention, repetition and novelty do not establish performance. Separating the reported event from its possible implication prevents a forecast from becoming an alleged fact.

## Procedure

1. State the decision horizon and question, such as which mechanisms might meet a thermal target under a mass constraint. Include the present baseline and a do-nothing option.
2. Build a query set using the required function, technical synonyms, competing mechanisms and failure constraints. Use publications, demonstrations, standards activity and supplier claims as distinct source roles.
3. Log query, service, filters and search date. Search outside the favored mechanism and record coverage gaps. A compact spreadsheet or Markdown table is sufficient.
4. For each signal, record the dated event, original source, quoted locator or careful paraphrase, application conditions, possible implication and uncertainty. Keep publication date distinct from event date and access date.
5. Trace repeated reports to their original evidence. Cluster related signals without counting repetitions as independent demonstrations.
6. Look for contrary signals: failed replication, unchanged performance under realistic loads, integration difficulty or an alternative explanation for increased attention.
7. Select a few signals for deeper landscape or mechanism work based on relevance and potential decision impact. Record why others are deferred. Set a review trigger rather than a perpetual scanning obligation.

## Output and validation

Each selected signal has a source, dated observation, tentative implication, contrary evidence and next question. A reviewer can reproduce the search scope. If the register is only names and predictions, return to the original events. No trend strength is inferred from an unnormalized count alone.

## Optional tools

Public scholarly indexes, patent-search portals and official technical announcements are discovery tools. A browser and table suffice. AI can suggest synonyms or clusters, but verify records and original passages; an AI summary is not another independent source.

## Limits and deeper knowledge

Unpublished work, indexing gaps, restricted sources and vocabulary changes limit coverage. This is a bounded scan, not evidence that absent search results mean absent technology.
