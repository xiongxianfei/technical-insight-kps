# Heilmeier Catechism

## Key takeaway

Challenge a research proposal through its objective, novelty, benefit, risk, resources and demonstrable milestones.

## Summary

Challenge a research proposal through its objective, novelty, benefit, risk, resources and demonstrable milestones. Use it for the stated output, not as a substitute for evidence or an entire engineering workflow.

## Established basis

**Technique:** Heilmeier Catechism. **Role:** analytical technique. The named technique is reused from [The Heilmeier Catechism](../references/darpa-undated-heilmeier-catechism.md). The worksheet, engineering example and local safeguards below are our exposition and application; they are not a new method, an official standard implementation or evidence that this workflow is effective.

## Inputs and prerequisites

A proposed research direction, beneficiary, current alternatives, tentative technical mechanism and decision about a bounded next investment.

## Rationale

A compelling idea is not yet an assessable proposal. Explicit baselines and tests reveal whether the claimed advance is meaningful and what uncertainty should be reduced first.

## Procedure

1. Explain the objective in plain language and identify how the need is handled today.
2. State what is different, why it could work and what evidence supports that mechanism; distinguish hope from demonstrated capability.
3. Identify who benefits, the magnitude and conditions of the benefit, and major technical and other risks.
4. Describe resource and schedule assumptions, plus intermediate and final measures that can reveal failure as well as success.
5. Use the answers to decide whether to investigate, reshape or stop the proposal; retain open technical questions.

## Worked example

**Synthetic example, not a measured investigation.** A proposed low-power monitor aims to preserve event detection while reducing transmissions. The novelty claim concerns a particular gating approach, not the existence of AI. A proposed milestone compares detection and energy on common traces. It remains a proposal; no test or funding decision is implied.

## Working template

| Objective and baseline | New approach and basis | Beneficiary and outcome | Risks | Resources and timing | Intermediate test | Final test |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plain language | Evidence versus hypothesis | Defined benefit | Explicit failures | Assumptions | Falsifiable result | Relevant demonstration |

## Output and validation

Deliver a concise research case with measurable, falsifiable milestones. Identify the weakest premise and what outcome would stop further investment.

## Limits and optional tools

The DARPA questions are a proposal-evaluation framework, not a causal validation method or a guarantee of funding, novelty or feasibility. Our table groups the questions for use; it is not an official submission form.

Paper or Markdown is sufficient unless the calculation or experiment requires more. A spreadsheet or established analysis package is optional; AI may suggest candidates but does not supply verified evidence, measurements or permissions. The downstream Practice selects and combines this technique; it need not be used in every investigation.
