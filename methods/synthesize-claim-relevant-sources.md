# Synthesize claim relevant sources

## Key takeaway

Compare what sources establish for a claim rather than counting how many documents mention it.

## Summary

Use this when important Principles, Models or Methods depend on outside knowledge. The result is a claim-source comparison with support, challenges, scope and an explicit inference to the proposed application.

## Inputs and prerequisites

A question and candidate claims, available source material and a record of inspection extent. One source can establish its own definition; broad causal or performance claims often require more than one kind of support.

## Rationale

Source authority, relevance and independence are different. Several documents may share an experiment. A standard supplies a norm within its authority, not proof that its choice is optimal everywhere.

## Procedure

1. Separate definition, association, mechanism, intervention-effect and design-choice claims. Identify what each requires.
2. Search for claim-relevant primary work and official technical guidance, including alternatives, contrary findings and boundary conditions.
3. Register publication identity, date basis, exact location and access extent. Do not turn abstract access into full-text evidence.
4. Extract the relevant contribution and what it does not establish. Trace shared datasets, studies or guidance to an evidence family.
5. Compare claims across sources; separate actual contradiction from different definitions, contexts, endpoints or assumptions.
6. Write the supported premises, unresolved gaps and your inference separately. Narrow the claim when the evidence does not support its original scope.
7. Stop when remaining uncertainty is sufficient for the intended decision or clearly requires a different kind of work. Record search scope and unresolved disagreement.
8. Update the affected Principle or Model and its local summaries; retain the source-specific records rather than copying all sources into every file.

## Expected effect

A reader can trace each important conclusion to located source contributions or an explicitly labelled inference.

## Validation and counterevidence

Test the synthesis with a contrary source and inspect whether the conclusion changes appropriately. If the conclusion remains unchanged regardless of the evidence, the process is rationalization. A desk synthesis is not an exhaustive systematic review.

## Limits

No fixed citation count guarantees quality. Different sources can have shared biases even when independent. External-source claims do not validate this package’s complete workflow.
