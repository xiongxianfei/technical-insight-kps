# Assess transfer to a new context

## Key takeaway

Compare source and target assumptions before adopting the same action.

## Summary

Use this when an insight, model or successful Method moves across products, scales, teams or disciplines. A reusable principle can remain useful while the concrete implementation changes.

## Inputs and prerequisites

Source insight and evidence envelope, target purpose/configuration, known constraints and authority to evaluate the target. Missing source conditions are a gap, not implicit similarity.

## Rationale

Validation has a bounded domain. Similar surface terminology can conceal different mechanisms, feedback, materials, distributions or consequences.

## Procedure

1. Restate the source claim with conditions and the evidence that supported it.
2. Define the target task and compare mechanisms, scale, units, environment, interfaces, people and consequences.
3. Mark each material assumption preserved, changed or unknown, with reasons.
4. Separate the transferable relationship from source-specific settings, timing, thresholds and tools.
5. Choose direct bounded reuse, a safe pilot, analysis/surrogate work or rejection. Define what would show transfer failed.
6. Evaluate in the target and update the local conclusion without rewriting the source history as if conditions had been identical.

## Expected effect

A target-specific application argument with explicit gaps and validation needs.

## Validation and counterevidence

Check that observed target outcomes include the original goal and material harms. Failure in the target can delimit applicability without proving the source observation false.

## Limits

Cross-domain analogies generate hypotheses; they do not supply mechanism evidence. High-consequence target use may require independent expert assessment even when the source evidence is strong.
