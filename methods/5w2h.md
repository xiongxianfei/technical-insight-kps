# 5W2H

## Key takeaway

Clarify a situation through seven concrete questions before choosing an explanation or solution.

## Summary

Clarify a situation through seven concrete questions before choosing an explanation or solution. Use it for the stated output, not as a substitute for evidence or an entire engineering workflow.

## Established basis

**Technique:** 5W2H. **Role:** analytical technique. The named technique is reused from [5W2H](../references/ahrq-undated-5w2h.md). The worksheet, engineering example and local safeguards below are our exposition and application; they are not a new method, an official standard implementation or evidence that this workflow is effective.

## Inputs and prerequisites

One situation, an intended decision, accessible observations and a person able to clarify unknowns. Do not collect private information merely to fill a worksheet.

## Rationale

A question can hide missing scope, timing, scale and ownership. Making these explicit separates a report from the assumptions attached to it.

## Procedure

1. State the situation in one sentence without presupposing a cause.
2. Ask Who is affected or knows the process; What happens; When it occurs; Where the boundary is; Why it matters; How it currently happens or is observed; How much or how many with units.
3. For each answer mark observed, reported, assumed or unknown. Attach its source or observation location.
4. Turn significant unknowns into evidence requests with an owner and a next check. Do not invent an answer to complete the form.
5. Write a bounded problem statement and decide which question needs causal analysis, comparison or further search.

## Worked example

**Synthetic example, not a measured investigation.** Who: build maintainers. What: one manifest test fails. When: repository checkout. Where: CI fixture. Why: blocks publication. How: compare listed and verified paths. How much: one failing check, runtime effect unknown. The resulting question concerns the file-set discrepancy; it does not yet assert that Git internals caused it.

## Working template

| Prompt | Answer and units | Evidence status | Source or next check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who What When Where Why How How much | Fill one row per prompt | Observed / reported / assumed / unknown | Located evidence or owner |

## Output and validation

Deliver a scoped statement plus an unknowns list. Another reader should distinguish the observed event, purpose, magnitude and open causes. A specific unknown is a valid output.

## Limits and optional tools

The Why prompt can concern purpose or suspected cause; label which one is meant. The AHRQ source is healthcare-process guidance; cross-domain engineering use is an application, not evidence of universal efficacy. A complete form does not validate a hypothesis.

Paper or Markdown is sufficient unless the calculation or experiment requires more. A spreadsheet or established analysis package is optional; AI may suggest candidates but does not supply verified evidence, measurements or permissions. The downstream Practice selects and combines this technique; it need not be used in every investigation.
