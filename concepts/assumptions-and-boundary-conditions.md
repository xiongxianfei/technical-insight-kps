# Assumptions and boundary conditions

## Key takeaway

An explanation is conditional on what was assumed and on the context in which its evidence applies.

## Summary

An assumption is a proposition accepted for a particular analysis without being established by that analysis. Boundary conditions delimit the system, operation, environment and use. A limit is where the account may no longer be dependable.

## Definition and distinctions

Separate fixed physical or logical constraints from empirical approximations, unknown inputs and chosen preferences. Record which are independently checked, estimated or merely supposed. A stated assumption is visible but not thereby justified.

## Worked distinction

A one-temperature thermal approximation assumes the internal temperature differences are negligible for the decision. Constant input power and constant heat-transfer resistance are extra conditions. A fitted time constant does not prove those conditions.

## Limits

An exhaustive assumption list is neither possible nor necessary. Focus on assumptions whose failure changes a conclusion or action. When transferring a result, revisit context rather than carrying an old confidence label forward.
