# Tradeoff

## Key takeaway

A design preference depends on objectives and constraints as well as on technical facts.

## Summary

A tradeoff arises when alternatives differ across relevant outcomes or conditions. It does not mean every restriction is negotiable. Mandatory safety, authorization and legal constraints bound the candidate set before preference scoring.

## Definition and distinctions

List hard constraints, desired outcomes, uncertainty and the consequences of being wrong. Distinguish measured performance, estimated cost and stakeholder preference. Inspect sensitivity to uncertain inputs rather than hiding them in one score.

## Worked distinction

More thermal storage may help short bursts but add mass and not reduce the eventual temperature rise. Better steady-state heat removal may help continuous operation but cost noise or power. The preferred alternative depends on use.

## Limits

Weights are judgments, not physical constants. Scores cannot establish an effect that has not been measured. Different legitimate objectives can produce different choices from the same evidence.
