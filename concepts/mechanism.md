# Mechanism

## Key takeaway

A mechanism explains the intermediate process connecting a condition or intervention to an outcome.

## Summary

A mechanism names entities, interactions and event relationships rather than replacing a cause with a technical-sounding word. Depending on the domain it can be physical, computational, informational, human or a combination.

## Definition and distinctions

Specify what changes first, how that alters an intermediate state, and how the measured outcome follows. Compare a mechanism with a black-box predictor: either can be useful, but good prediction alone does not prove the proposed internal story.

## Worked distinction

In an illustrative manifest failure, generator inclusion of Git files produces a larger listed-path set than the validator’s payload set. Set disagreement produces the coverage error. This is more informative than “CI is flaky.” In a thermal model, storage and transfer are distinct terms in an energy balance.

## Limits

Do not label every arrow in a diagram measured. Multiple mechanisms can produce the same output. Knowledge of a mechanism in one context does not establish its dominance in another.
