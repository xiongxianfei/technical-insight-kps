# Technical insight

## Key takeaway

An insight is a scoped explanatory understanding that changes what you can predict, investigate or decide.

## Summary

Technical Insight KPS uses this as a working definition, not an industry standard. An insight connects a phenomenon, a proposed relationship or mechanism, supporting evidence and consequences for action. It may reveal an opportunity, not only explain a failure. A surprising sentence, a working fix and a new idea can each be valuable without yet meeting that definition.

## Definition and distinctions

A useful insight record says: in context C, changing X is expected to affect Y through M, on basis E, subject to limits L. Some insights concern measurement, constraints or representation rather than a physical causal mechanism. A negative finding can be an insight when it reliably eliminates a consequential possibility.

## Worked distinction

A simulated enclosure can be cooler early because it stores more heat, while having the same eventual temperature rise. This suggests distinguishing transient and steady-state objectives. “Add a heat sink” is a candidate solution, not the insight; “the trace is lower” is an observation.

## Limits

Insight maturity and practical adoption are separate. A plausible mechanism may be untested; a reliable intervention may work for reasons that remain uncertain. Novelty to one engineer is not novelty to a field. No self-assigned confidence label certifies truth.
