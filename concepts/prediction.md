# Prediction

## Key takeaway

A prediction states an expected observable consequence before examining the result used to assess it.

## Summary

Predictions connect explanations to measurements. They may be qualitative ordering, a quantitative interval, a timing relation or a boundary case. State the context and tolerance needed to distinguish accounts.

## Definition and distinctions

A useful prediction has a variable, unit or event definition, operating condition and contrary signal. Separate prediction data from data used to choose or calibrate a model. Discovery on an existing dataset is valid exploratory work, not automatically prospective confirmation.

## Worked distinction

The same thermal model predicts a lower temperature at 100 seconds with greater storage but an unchanged limiting temperature if resistance and power remain fixed. A statement that “the curve will change” is too weak to distinguish storage from heat removal.

## Limits

An accurate prediction may be shared by rival models. A numerical point without uncertainty can be misleading. Retrospective explanations should be identified as retrospective.
