# Hypothesis

## Key takeaway

A hypothesis is a candidate account with consequences that can be compared with evidence.

## Summary

A hypothesis can explain an anomaly, forecast a design improvement or identify a measurement artifact. It becomes useful when its assumptions and distinctive predictions are explicit. “The system is complicated” does not specify a discriminating consequence.

## Definition and distinctions

Distinguish descriptive hypotheses about an association, mechanistic hypotheses about a process and intervention hypotheses about a change. State at least one material alternative when it exists. An untested alternative is not eliminated by confidence in a favourite account.

## Worked distinction

For a slower temperature rise, candidates include increased thermal storage, reduced power input, improved heat removal and changed sensor dynamics. They need not be mutually exclusive. Early readings alone may be compatible with all four.

## Limits

Failed predictions can reflect implementation, measurement or auxiliary-assumption failures as well as the central explanation. A test only challenges the account actually operationalized. Hypotheses are not required to be novel.
