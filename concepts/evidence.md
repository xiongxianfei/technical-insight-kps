# Evidence

## Key takeaway

Evidence is a contribution to a particular claim, not a synonym for a citation or an attractive result.

## Summary

A reading, experiment, source passage, derivation or counterexample becomes evidential when its relation to a claim is specified. Evidence can support, weaken, constrain or leave a claim unresolved. Its scope and independence matter.

## Definition and distinctions

Record the claim, the observed or reported material, the inference connecting them and the relevant limitations. A standard establishes its requirements within its authority; it does not prove every mandated choice optimal. A repeated analysis of the same data is not a new independent experiment.

## Worked distinction

A formula and a numerical calculation support a conditional prediction of a chosen model. They do not establish that the model describes a real product. An experiment can support an effect under tested conditions while leaving the mechanism uncertain.

## Limits

Source prestige, number of citations and reproducible processing do not substitute for relevance. No evidence is “strong” without saying strong for which claim. Missing data and publication selection can affect a synthesis.
